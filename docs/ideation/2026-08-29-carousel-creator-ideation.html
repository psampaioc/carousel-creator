Carousel Creator: a useful live-demo slice

A ranked set of directions for an Instagram-first tool for local newsletters and media. The stack is fixed: Next.js, Convex, and Vercel. The decision is which product promise is specific enough to be useful and contained enough to execute convincingly.

Focus: local social contentStack: Next.js · Convex · VercelOutput: human-reviewed carousel
Recommendation: build a constrained local-events storyboard, not a generic design editor. Use normal web layout—HTML and CSS rendered by Next.js—for the first slide system. A canvas, drag-and-drop editor, publishing automation, and AI event discovery would make the demo broader but less credible.

Grounding Context

The repo’s brief already sets the product boundary: structured local items become a consistent carousel; a person reviews copy and details before export; reusable templates, preview/editing, and Instagram-friendly export belong in the first version. Automatic fact verification, autonomous publishing, broad platform support, analytics, and heavyweight brand operations do not.

Your existing Simple MP3 Creator demonstrates an ability to ship a real web workflow with a Next.js front end, Vercel deployment, persistent jobs, clear state, and careful hosted validation. Here, that strength should show up as a finished create → review → export loop—not as a large feature count.

External grounding reinforces the distinction: Canva already offers a huge template catalog and a full drag-and-drop editor. The opportunity is a local-editorial workflow that makes information ready for publishing, rather than competing with a general-purpose design suite. Convex can safely hold drafts and images, but asset URLs need deliberate access handling; Vercel gives every Git change a preview URL, which is excellent for rehearsing and showing the demo.

Topic Axes

  • Editorial structure
    Turn a list into a story with a hook, useful sequence, and close.
  • Local-information quality
    Make dates, venues, sources, and images visible enough to check.
  • Review and trust
    Preserve human control instead of hiding uncertainty behind automation.
  • Demo legibility
    Show one crisp, end-to-end outcome that can be understood in minutes.
  • Reusable product foundation
    Use the fixed stack where persistence and deploy previews genuinely add value.

Ranked Ideas

1

Local Weekender

Make one strong promise: turn five vetted events into a 7-slide “This Weekend in Lisbon” carousel—cover, five event cards, and a closing slide. The user begins with structured event details, sees a real carousel immediately, edits it, and exports it.

Axis
Editorial structure
Basis
direct: the repository’s first named use case is “Top 10 events in [city]”; the discovery issue asks for structured fields, cover/item/closing slides, preview, editing, and export.
Rationale
It is recognizably valuable to a local publisher while sharply limiting the surface: one city, one format, one human-reviewed result.
Downsides
It deliberately does not solve discovery, scheduling, or every content format.

2

Editorial Readiness Rail

Give the draft a visible checklist that explains why a carousel is or is not ready to export: missing event date, no location, no source URL, missing image, or text that will overflow the selected slide layout. It should direct the user back to the exact item that needs attention.

Axis
Local-information quality
Basis
direct: the brief makes review mandatory before publishing and the issue explicitly asks how missing images, links, dates, or locations should be handled.
Rationale
This is the product’s defensible behaviour: it helps publishers avoid a bad local-information post, instead of merely making a pretty but unverified graphic.
Downsides
It is guardrails, not fact checking; the user still owns accuracy.
Confidence
91%
Complexity
Low
3

Format Cards, Not Blank Canvases

Start with a small set of editorial formats that encode storytelling choices: “This weekend,” “Top 5,” “Free events,” and “Family picks.” Each format says what the cover needs, what each item card shows, and how the closing slide behaves.

Axis
Reusable product foundation
Basis
external: Canva’s carousel catalog already offers thousands of templates plus a general editor; a small product wins by encoding a specific editorial job, not by matching catalog breadth.
Rationale
Format cards make later expansion cheap while keeping the first demo coherent and understandable.
Downsides
Early templates can feel restrictive if their tone and typography are not well chosen.

87%

4

Demo Mode Is a Product Surface

Ship one seeded example—“5 things happening in Lisbon this weekend”—that can be opened, edited, and exported without sign-up or empty-state setup. Treat it as a genuine product path, not a throwaway mock: the data model and rendering are the same ones a real draft uses.

Axis
Demo legibility
Basis
reasoned: a live demo is judged by the clarity of its end-to-end result. A real example lets the audience see the intended workflow before they have to understand configuration or author their own content.
Rationale
It gives you a dependable demonstration path while still proving that the system is a real app, not a static slideshow.
Downsides
The sample must remain explicitly labelled as illustrative and should not be presented as current event data.
Confidence
86%
Complexity
Low
5

Reviewable Drafts

Persist each carousel as a draft with a simple lifecycle: editing, needs-attention, ready-to-export, exported. The value of Convex is visible here: the side-by-side editor, carousel preview, and readiness state stay in sync without inventing collaboration or an entire CMS.

Axis
Review and trust
Basis
direct: the brief asks to preserve drafts and makes review a first-class step. Convex File Storage supports uploaded assets and metadata, while its documentation also makes the access-control boundary explicit.
Rationale
This creates a credible reason for persistence and a clean future path to teams, without requiring those features now.
Downsides
Do not turn draft states into a complex workflow engine or expose private asset URLs casually.
Confidence
82%
Complexity
Medium
6

DOM-first Slide System

Represent each slide as normal application content rendered with HTML and CSS in Next.js: title, metadata, image, accent, and a small fixed set of layouts. The preview itself is the design surface; introduce a freeform canvas only when the real workflow proves it is needed.

Axis
Reusable product foundation
Basis
reasoned: a structured local-event card has stable fields and repeatable layouts. A DOM layout gives fast iteration, responsive preview, accessibility, and predictable visual constraints; a canvas solves freedom that the chosen wedge does not yet need.
Rationale
It lets the fixed Next.js/Convex/Vercel stack carry a polished, accessible UI instead of consuming the demo with editor mechanics.
Downsides
Export fidelity needs a later, deliberate rendering decision; do not overclaim that browser preview alone is a production image pipeline.
Confidence
80%
Complexity
Medium

Rejection Summary

1Full Canva-style freeform editorDuplicates established general-purpose tooling and makes editor mechanics, not local editorial usefulness, the demo.
2AI event discoveryConflicts with the stated human-review boundary and raises freshness, provenance, and verification questions before the core workflow exists.
3Direct Instagram publishing and schedulingExplicitly out of scope; manual export is enough to demonstrate useful output.
4Campaign calendar workspaceInteresting later, but requires multiple drafts and editorial planning before one draft is proven valuable.
5Template marketplaceRequires a creator ecosystem and broad template infrastructure, neither of which establishes the initial product value.
6Per-slide collaboration commentsReview readiness is more valuable than multi-user discussion for a first local-publisher demo.
7Cross-platform adaptationInstagram-first is a productive constraint; early multi-platform support dilutes the selected format.
8Analytics and engagement scoringNeeds published-content data and a stable product loop; it cannot validate the first live demonstration.
